Culture, art and heritage.

Addis Ababa is home to people from many communities and backgrounds. Their languages, traditions, creativity and histories contribute to the character of the city.

ባህልCulture · Bahil
Many voices

A city of many cultures

As Ethiopia's capital, Addis Ababa brings together people from across the country and beyond. Different languages, customs, beliefs and ways of life can be experienced throughout the city.

This diversity is visible in everyday life, from markets and neighborhoods to food, music, celebrations and public spaces.

ሙዚቃ

Music and dance

Music and dance are important forms of cultural expression in Ethiopia. Styles differ across communities and regions, with distinctive rhythms, instruments, movements and performance traditions.

In Addis Ababa, traditional music exists alongside contemporary Ethiopian music and other international influences.

ጥበብ

Art and creativity

The city's creative life includes painting, sculpture, photography, crafts, design and contemporary artistic practices. Artists use different materials and ideas to express personal experiences and explore social and cultural themes.

Galleries, cultural spaces, workshops and public events provide opportunities to discover creative work.

ልብስ

Traditional clothing and craft

Traditional Ethiopian clothing includes garments made from woven cotton, often decorated with patterns and borders. Styles and designs vary across communities and occasions.

Weaving, embroidery, basketry, pottery and other crafts are part of the country's diverse artistic heritage.

ታሪክ

History and heritage

Addis Ababa has a relatively recent history compared with many historic cities, but it has played an important role in Ethiopia's modern political, diplomatic and cultural life.

Historic buildings, monuments, museums and public spaces help tell stories about the city's development and the people who shaped it.

Travel thoughtfully

Respecting cultural diversity

Culture is living and constantly changing. When learning about traditions, it is important to avoid treating one community's practices as representative of everyone.

Listen to local voices, use reliable sources, and ask permission before photographing people or cultural activities.
